# The fuel that keeps the Ratchet moving

Pack 177 v1 made a real finite calculation, but it let that small calibration
become the center of the project. That was a semantic deletion. The Ratchet
needs three different fuels, and they must remain separate.

## 1. Operational fuel: measured residual pressure

At tick `r`, the Ratchet has a frozen quotient, candidate frontier, controls,
and history. Its next work is not chosen by a story. It is generated by a
counterexample or an unresolved distinction:

\[
\Delta_r=
(c_r,k_r,u_r,g_r),
\]

where, source by source:

- `c` counts contexts that the current returned coordinates identify even
  though their observed continuations differ;
- `k` counts candidates killed by later held-out records;
- `u` records plural surviving completions or plural minimal quotients; and
- `g` records missing or failed source-runtime interfaces.

This is a vector, not a score. No weighted sum ranks sources or mathematical
families. Each nonzero component generates a typed obligation, and a fair
scheduler eventually offers every obligation.

The finite root analogue of the owner's phrase "entropy escaping" is:

\[
x\sim_r y
\quad\text{but}\quad
\Lambda_h(x)\ne\Lambda_h(y).
\]

The current quotient treats two occurrences alike, while an admitted finite
continuation exposes a difference. The Ratchet may answer by refining a
coordinate, lengthening history, retaining a relation instead of a function,
enlarging the carrier so the exit becomes internal, or holding for a missing
measurement. It may not invent a preferred answer.

This operational residual is not automatically thermodynamic entropy. The
owner's physical entropy-gradient hypothesis is kept separately in the owner
hypothesis reservoir.

## 2. Proposal fuel: a plural construction ecology

Residuals do not design every useful candidate by themselves. The proposal
reservoir supplies plausible next constructions from:

- existing repository simulations and negative results;
- finite relations, automata, constraint systems, tilings, and gluing;
- ordered and bracketed histories;
- spinor, Clifford, division-algebra, spectral, and nonassociative carriers;
- finite Feynman/Dirac constructions;
- QIT, FEP, JEPA-like predictive models, memory, and control systems;
- biological convergence and assembly problems; and
- the owner's proposed physics and engine architecture.

These entries are fuel because they reduce blind combinatorial search. They do
not become evidence merely by being proposed. `fuel/FUEL_REGISTRY.json` gives
each entry a role, assumption boundary, executable surface, and evidence
license. Entries have no hidden priority score.

## 3. Owner-hypothesis fuel: a precise high-value map

The owner's hypotheses are not generic metaphors. They are unusually detailed
candidate structures developed across the estate. Hiding them is not
agnosticism; it destroys proposal fuel and guarantees rediscovery by accident.

They therefore remain explicit and accurately stated in
`OWNER_HYPOTHESIS_FUEL_MAP.md`. They may seed typed candidates and sealed
comparisons. Their labels, desired dimensions, stage counts, physics names,
and symbolic correspondences are forbidden from the mathematical selector.

## Fair consumption

`source/compile_fuel_obligations.py` executes the first fuel interface. It
reads the already executed anonymous r3-r6 receipts, compiles a residual vector
and concrete obligations for every source, and emits a round-robin projection
for Leviathan. The queue order is logistical only. Leviathan may schedule,
resume, event, and receipt this queue; it may not decide mathematical truth.

The broad proposal registry is also emitted as an unranked proposal queue.
Each lane remains `PROPOSE`, `RUN`, `HOLD`, `FAIL`, or `PARK` until its own
typed execution changes that state.

## What is retained

No donor was removed. This v2 pack adds both Pack 175 returns to the four
donors already preserved by v1. More importantly, it restores the information
that was semantically compressed away: why the Ratchet has a next move, what
construction families may answer it, and what the owner's actual proposed
attractor looks like.
