## Specific Object-Class Corrections

### Clifford Torus vs Clifford Module

The master atlas uses:

```text
T_(pi/4)
```

as the Clifford torus: the symmetric torus stratum inside the nested Hopf-torus family. This is a geometric surface.

The 13-layer fixture uses:

```text
clifford_module_geometry
```

as an algebra/module layer. This is not the same object. Pauli matrices can witness noncommutation and can generate matrix algebras under admitted closure, but closure to a Clifford module is not free under the no-closure-by-default rule. If a sim needs a Clifford module, it must name the additional algebraic closure/admission step.

### Cl(p,q) Basin Framing

F01/N01 are too weak to force a unique Clifford algebra or signature. They admit finite noncommutative structures; they do not exclude Heisenberg-Weyl, generalized Weyl/clock-shift, finite Pauli, quantum-group, fuzzy-sphere, C*-algebra/GNS, or other finite operator-algebra branches.

The Cl(p,q) attractor-basin program should be retired as the default goal. It may be revived only as:

```text
F01 + N01 + explicit extra constraint X -> candidate Clifford selection
```

where `X` is named and tested, such as a quadratic form/signature axiom, spectral-triple/Dirac data, Lindblad dissipative selector, or quantum-principal-bundle coaction.

The repo already contains one candidate `X`: the source-backed Lindblad terrain/composition system. Treat it as an existing candidate to test, not as a missing invention and not as a proved selector. The next admissible algebra-level question is whether the recorded terrain/composition system selects:

- a Clifford-class fixed-observable/steady-state algebra;
- a plural admissible algebra family;
- a quantum-channel or quantum-semigroup structure that is not well described as Clifford.

### Ground State vs Algebra-Level Tests

Hamiltonian ground-state stabilizer tests are state-level tests. They are not decisive falsifiers of an algebra-level emergence claim. Existing TFIM/MPS/DMRG results should be read as:

```text
ground-state stabilizers reflect the tested Hamiltonian symmetry
```

not as:

```text
the algebra-of-observables question is closed
```

If an algebra-level question is asked, test the generated observable algebra, steady-state Lindbladian algebra, or explicitly selected algebra family.

For the recorded source-backed system, the relevant observable is not a TFIM ground state. It is the algebra generated or stabilized by the 8 terrain Lindblad/Hamiltonian laws under the nested schedule composition, with comparison against Cl, Heisenberg-Weyl, SU(2)/Pauli, C*-algebra/GNS, and quantum-semigroup candidates.
