## 4. Derived Constraints

Derived constraints are anti-smuggling fences. They block classical primitives
from entering before they are earned.

### 4.1 No Primitive Identity

Do not assume objects are self-identical in an absolute way.

Operational form:

```text

```

```text

different coordinate chart but same operational state
same entropy but different trace distance
```

### 4.2 No Primitive Equality

Do not use equality as free substitution.

Operational form:

```text
a == b only relative to P if no probe in P distinguishes them
```

Controls:

```text

```

### 4.3 No Primitive Probability

Do not start with a classical sample space.

QIT form:

```text

```

Probability appears only with a named effect or probe. It is not a free
primitive distribution.

### 4.4 No Primitive Time Or Causality

Do not start with a wall clock or total causal order.

Operational replacement:

```text

path families

```

### 4.5 No Primitive Metric Or Coordinates

Coordinate distance is not physical content.

Operational replacement:

```text

```

### 4.6 No Closure By Default

Do not assume a family of operations forms a group or algebra.

Example:

```text

amplitude damping is CPTP but not invertible
```

Every closure claim needs a closure test.

### 4.7 No Outside Observer

The observer/probe is part of the same substrate.

Operational form:

```text

trace out one side only after specifying the cut
back-action and cut choice are part of the model
```

### 4.8 No Global Total Order

Do not rank all states by one scalar.

Entropy, purity, free energy, and mutual information are readouts, not total
orders.

Controls:

```text

different scalar value but indistinguishable under active probes
```

### 4.9 No Semantic Smuggling

Do not rename a classical idea and call it QIT-aligned.

Example:

```text

coherent information can be negative
```

So `I_c` is not "just quantum mutual information." It has different behavior
and must be tested as its own object.
