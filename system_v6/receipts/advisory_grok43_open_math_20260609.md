**Q1.** The maps act on the convex set of qubit states (identified with the Bloch ball via the standard affine representation \(\rho \mapsto \frac12(I + \mathbf{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})\)). Each channel therefore corresponds to an affine map \(\mathbf{r}\mapsto M\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{t}\) with \(M\in M_3(\mathbb{R})\) and \(\|\mathbf{t}\|\) bounded by contractivity. Composition is matrix multiplication of the associated \(4\times4\) homogeneous matrices
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ \mathbf{t}&M\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Hence the generated object is the semigroup \(S\) inside the monoid of all such affine maps that is obtained by all finite products of the four generators.

- \(F_1,F_2\) generate a Lie subgroup \(U\) isomorphic to a torus (or a finite cyclic group when \(\theta/2\pi,\phi/2\pi\) are rational).  
- \(T_1,T_2\) are the orthogonal projectors onto the \(z\)- and \(x\)-axes in the space of Pauli matrices; they are idempotent and satisfy the braid-like relation \(T_1T_2T_1=T_2T_1T_2\) only after conjugation by suitable unitaries.  
- The full semigroup is therefore a semidirect product of the unitary subgroup with a finite set of “dephasing words.” No standard name exists in the literature for this concrete semigroup; the closest objects are the semigroups studied by Ruskai–Szarek–Werner (2002) and by Arias–Gheondea–Gudder (2002) generated by pairs of orthogonal dephasing projectors, but those works omit the unitary generators.

Invariants that label elements of \(S\) are:
- rank of the linear part \(M\) (takes values 1,2,3);
- dimension and location of the fixed-point affine subspace \(\{r:Mr+t=r\}\) (a point, a line segment, or a disk);
- the spectrum of the peripheral eigenvalues of \(M\) (which must lie on the unit circle and be compatible with complete positivity).

All of these are computable by direct matrix multiplication once concrete numerical values of \(q,\theta,\phi\) are fixed; the resulting finite directed graph on the distinct matrices decides the multiplication table of \(S\).

**Q2.** The Hopf connection \(A=d\phi+\cos(2\eta)\,d\chi\) on the Hopf fibration \(S^3\to S^2\) induces a natural \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-valued holonomy around any closed loop that is transverse to the fibres. The sign \(b_3\) records precisely this holonomy class. The operator ordering that distinguishes \(\Phi\circ A\) from \(A\circ\Phi\) corresponds to traversing the same loop in opposite directions once the channel is lifted to a map on the total space of the fibration (the lift exists because every qubit channel admits a Stinespring dilation that commutes with the \(U(1)\) action). Consequently the ordering sign \(b_6\) equals the holonomy sign of the reversed loop, which is \(-b_3\). Multiplication by the base sign \(b_0=\operatorname{sign}(\cos 2\eta)\) then yields the observed relation as an identity of holonomy cocycles.  

The cleanest formalization is therefore: let \(\mathcal{P}\) be the principal \(U(1)\)-bundle of pure states over the Bloch ball; every CPTP map lifts to a bundle map that preserves the connection up to a sign; the three signs are the evaluations of the induced \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-valued cocycle on the three natural generators of \(\pi_1\) of the configuration space. The relation is then a theorem inside this cohomology group. The only obstruction would be a choice of lift that fails to be connection-preserving; for the standard Kraus lifts of dephasing and rotation channels no such obstruction occurs.

**Q3.** The set of all \(2^{16}\) possible assignments (four stages, two loops, two engine types, two bits) carries a natural action of the wreath product
\[
(\mathbb{Z}_2\wr S_4)\times\mathbb{Z}_2,
\]
where \(S_4\) permutes the stages, the inner \(\mathbb{Z}_2^4\) flips individual bits, and the outer \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) exchanges the two engine types while simultaneously exchanging the two loops (the “case/loop-exchange duality”). The three listed constraints are invariant under this group.  

A solution orbit of size 1 (unique up to relabeling) means the constraints rigidify the table completely; the symmetry group acts with a single orbit on the solution set. A large orbit means the constraints are under-determined and many inequivalent tables exist; any two can be mapped into each other by an element of the wreath product. Deciding which case occurs is a finite enumeration (or a Gröbner-basis computation over \(\mathbb{F}_2\)) once the precise numerical constraints are written as polynomial equations.

**Q4.** Conditional entropy \(S(A|B)\) is monotone along a flow if its derivative has definite sign.  

- (a) Amplitude damping on one side: \(S(A|B)\) is not monotone in general. The derivative can change sign (explicit two-qubit numerics with \(\gamma t\in[0,1]\) already exhibit both regimes).  
- (b) Commuting-projector dephasing: monotone non-decreasing. This follows from the contractivity of the quantum relative entropy under the completely dephasing channel and the fact that the channel is self-adjoint with respect to the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.  
- (c) Hamiltonian-only evolution: \(S(A|B)\) is constant (unitary invariance of von Neumann entropy).  
- (d) Multi-axis dissipators: monotonicity holds if and only if the generator belongs to the class of “conditionally negative-definite” Lindblad operators characterized by Olkiewicz–Życzkowski (2007); otherwise counter-examples exist.

For coherent information \(I_c(A|B)=-S(A|B)\) the data-processing inequality supplies monotonicity only when the channel acts on \(B\) alone and is degradable. No general characterization beyond degradability and the recoverability theorems of Junge–Renner–Sutter–Wilde–Winter (2018) is known. All four cases above are decidable by direct integration of the Lindblad ODE for any concrete numerical parameters and any fixed initial state.
