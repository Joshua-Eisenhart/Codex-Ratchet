## B. Standard Anchors

Label: `ASSISTANT-GLOSS / external-standard`. These are definitions used to shape a finite discriminator candidate, not doctrine.

- A twistor is usually written as a pair of two spinors. In the user's convention, `Z^alpha = (omega^A, pi_A')`; in Adamo's lecture-note convention, `Z^A = (mu^{dot alpha}, lambda_alpha)`. The notation differs, but the carrier idea is the same: four homogeneous complex coordinates split into two spinor pieces. Source: Adamo, "Lectures on twistor theory", arXiv `1712.02196`, lines 589-607.
- Projective twistor space is modeled by homogeneous coordinates in `CP^3`, with nonzero scalar rescaling identified. Source: Adamo, lines 554-590; De Haro/Butterfield philosophical review PDF lines 364-401.
- The basic incidence relation is linear. In one convention it is `mu^{dot alpha} = x^{alpha dot alpha} lambda_alpha`; in Lorentzian convention Adamo adds a factor `i`; in the user's convention this is the requested `omega^A = i x^{AA'} pi_A'` form. Source: Adamo, lines 601-607 and 788-802.
- A spacetime point corresponds to a projective line `CP^1` in twistor space under the incidence relation. Source: Adamo, lines 630-681.
- Two such twistor lines intersect iff the corresponding points are null separated. Source: Adamo, lines 705-724 and 757-760.
- A point in projective twistor space corresponds to an alpha-plane/totally null two-plane in complexified spacetime. Source: Adamo, lines 710-724.

- Survives: homogeneous/projective quotient; finite projective points as one-dimensional subspaces; finite projective lines as two-dimensional subspaces or point sets; incidence membership; line intersection graph; quotient checks; scramble/random controls; finite reconstruction-from-incidence tests.
