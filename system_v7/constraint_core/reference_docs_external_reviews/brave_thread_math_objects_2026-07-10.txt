Key properties and developments include:

Asymptotic Significance: Research by Hiai and Petz (1991) established that Umegaki's relative entropy is the asymptotic exponent in quantum hypothesis testing, linking it to the distinguishability of quantum states in the limit of many copies. 
Monotonicity: It satisfies the data processing inequality, meaning it is non-increasing under trace-preserving positive linear maps. This monotonicity is closely related to the strong subadditivity of von Neumann entropy.

Integral Formulas: Recent work (2023) has provided new integral formulas relating Umegaki's relative entropy to trace norms, offering simpler proofs of fundamental inequalities like the Holevo bound.

octonians?


Octonions (often denoted as $\mathbb{O}$) are an 8-dimensional normed division algebra over the real numbers, representing the largest of the four such algebras (following real numbers, complex numbers, and quaternions).  They are also known as Cayley numbers.

Key Properties
Octonions extend the quaternions but lose another algebraic property in the process. While real numbers are commutative and associative, and quaternions are associative but non-commutative, octonions are non-commutative and non-associative.  However, they satisfy a weaker condition known as alternativity ($x(xy) = (xx)y$ and $(yx)x = y(xx)$) and are power associative. 

Their multiplication rules for the seven imaginary units ($e_1$ through $e_7$) are often visualized using the Fano plane, a projective geometry diagram where lines indicate the cyclic multiplication order of the basis elements.

Construction and History
Discovered independently by John T. Graves in 1843 and Arthur Cayley in 1845, octonions are constructed via the Cayley-Dickson process, which doubles the dimension of the previous algebra (quaternions).  According to Hurwitz's theorem, no further normed division algebras exist beyond 8 dimensions; extending this process yields sedenions (16 dimensions), which contain zero divisors and lose the division algebra property. 

Applications in Physics and Mathematics
Despite their complexity, octonions are crucial in understanding exceptional structures in mathematics and theoretical physics:

Exceptional Lie Groups: The automorphism group of the octonions is the exceptional Lie group $G_2$.  Other exceptional groups ($F_4, E_6, E_7, E_8$) are intimately linked to octonionic projective planes.
String Theory: Octonions appear naturally in superstring theory and M-theory, particularly regarding why these theories often require 10 or 11 spacetime dimensions (related to the spinor representations of $Spin(9,1)$). 
Standard Model: Recent research, notably by Cohl Furey, explores using octonionic algebra to derive the symmetries of the Standard Model of particle physics, attempting to unify fundamental forces.

Umegaki relative-entropy work with octonians?


Umegaki relative entropy does not directly apply to octonions in its standard form because the definition relies on the algebraic structure of associative von Neumann algebras or matrix algebras over complex numbers, whereas octonions are non-associative. 

Fundamental Obstacle: Non-Associativity
The standard definition of Umegaki relative entropy for two states $\rho$ and $\sigma$ is:
S(ρ∥σ)=Tr(ρ(logρ−logσ))
This formula implicitly assumes:

Associativity: The trace operation and the functional calculus (like $\log \sigma$) rely on the associative property of the underlying algebra to ensure well-defined spectral decomposition and operator multiplication. 
Hilbert Space Structure: Standard quantum mechanics and von Neumann algebra theory are built on complex Hilbert spaces where operators form an associative algebra. Octonions, being non-associative, do not form a von Neumann algebra in the traditional sense. 
Current Research Status
No Direct Extension: There is no widely accepted, standard definition of Umegaki relative entropy specifically for octonionic quantum mechanics. The lack of associativity complicates the definition of the logarithm of an octonionic operator and the trace properties required for the relative entropy to satisfy monotonicity (data processing inequality). 
Generalized Contexts: While Araki and Uhlmann extended relative entropy to general von Neumann algebras (which are associative), extending these concepts to non-associative algebras like the octonions remains a niche and highly theoretical area.  Some research explores "non-associative quantum mechanics," but a rigorous information-theoretic framework analogous to Umegaki's is not yet established.
Alternative Approaches: In contexts involving octonions (such as certain string theory models or exceptional Lie group studies), researchers typically map octonionic structures into larger associative algebras (like matrix algebras over octonions, which require careful handling of associativity, e.g., using Jordan algebras) before applying standard entropy measures. For instance, the exceptional Jordan algebra ($H_3(\mathbb{O})$) is associative in the Jordan sense (power associative and commutative, but not fully associative), and entropy measures have been studied in this specific Jordan algebraic context, but this differs from the direct Umegaki definition.

How do Jordan algebras handle octonion entropy?


Jordan algebras handle octonion entropy by providing an associative-enough framework (specifically power associativity) that allows for the definition of spectral functions like the logarithm, which are impossible to define consistently in the raw non-associative octonion algebra. 

The primary structure used is the Exceptional Jordan Algebra (also known as the Albert Algebra, denoted $H_3(\mathbb{O})$), which consists of $3 \times 3$ Hermitian matrices with octonionic entries. 

Mechanism: Overcoming Non-Associativity
Raw octonions fail to support the standard Umegaki entropy formula $S(\rho | \sigma) = \text{Tr}(\rho (\log \rho - \log \sigma))$ because the lack of associativity makes the product $\rho \log \sigma$ ambiguous and the spectral theorem unreliable. Jordan algebras resolve this through:

Jordan Product: Instead of the standard matrix product $AB$, the algebra uses the symmetrized product $A \circ B = \frac{1}{2}(AB + BA)$. While $H_3(\mathbb{O})$ is not fully associative, it is power associative, meaning expressions like $A^n$ and functions of a single operator $f(A)$ (such as $\log A$) are well-defined. 
Trace Linearity: The trace function on $H_3(\mathbb{O})$ is linear and real-valued. Crucially, while the full algebra is non-associative, the subalgebra generated by any two elements (if they associate) or the specific manipulations required for the trace of a single element's function remain consistent. 
State Definition: States are defined as positive linear functionals on the Jordan algebra. For $H_3(\mathbb{O})$, a state corresponds to a density matrix with real eigenvalues summing to 1, allowing the entropy to be calculated based on these eigenvalues.
Entropy Definition in this Context
In the context of the Albert Algebra, the von Neumann entropy (and by extension, relative entropy) is typically defined via the eigenvalues of the density matrices, bypassing the need for direct non-associative multiplication in the final scalar result:

Key Properties
Dimension: It has 27 real dimensions (3 real diagonal entries + 3 octonionic off-diagonal entries $\times$ 8 dimensions each = $3 + 24 = 27$).
Exceptionality: Unlike Jordan algebras derived from real, complex, or quaternionic matrices (which are "special"), the Albert algebra is exceptional because the non-associativity of the underlying octonions prevents it from being embedded into any associative algebra. 
Automorphism Group: The group of automorphisms preserving the Jordan product structure of the Albert algebra is the exceptional Lie group $F_4$.  The group of linear transformations preserving its determinant is the exceptional Lie group $E_6$. 
Significance in Physics
The Albert Algebra is central to attempts to unify the Standard Model of particle physics with gravity using geometric algebra:

Standard Model Symmetries: Research suggests that the gauge group of the Standard Model ($SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$) can be derived as the intersection of specific subgroups within $F_4$ that stabilize certain elements of the Albert Algebra. 
Generations of Fermions: The 27-dimensional structure is often hypothesized to correspond to the three generations of fermions (each generation having distinct properties), offering a potential algebraic explanation for why there are exactly three generations. 
Spacetime Geometry: The algebra decomposes naturally into parts that can be identified with 10-dimensional spacetime and spinors, linking it to superstring theory and M-theory.

How does F4 relate to particle physics?

Derivation of Standard Model Symmetries
The gauge group of the Standard Model, $SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$ (modulo a discrete subgroup), can be derived directly from $F_4$ without arbitrary input.

Intersection of Subgroups: Research shows that the Standard Model gauge group arises as the intersection of two maximal subgroups within $F_4$:
$Spin(9)$: Related to spacetime symmetries in 10 dimensions (relevant to string theory).
$SU(3) \times SU(3) / \mathbb{Z}_3$: Related to the internal color and flavor symmetries. 
Stabilizer Groups: By identifying specific elements in the Albert Algebra (such as idempotents representing projection operators), the subgroup of $F_4$ that leaves these elements invariant corresponds exactly to the known forces acting on particles.

g2 vs f4


The primary difference between $G_2$ and $F_4$ lies in the algebraic structures they preserve: $G_2$ is the automorphism group of the octonions ($\mathbb{O}$), while $F_4$ is the automorphism group of the Albert Algebra ($H_3(\mathbb{O})$). 

1. Fundamental Definitions
G2: The smallest exceptional Lie group (14-dimensional). It consists of all transformations that preserve the multiplication table of the octonions. Since octonions are 8-dimensional, $G_2$ acts naturally on the 7 imaginary dimensions. 
F4: The next smallest exceptional Lie group (52-dimensional). It consists of all transformations that preserve the Jordan product of the Albert Algebra (the $3 \times 3$ Hermitian octonionic matrices). It contains $G_2$ as a subgroup.

2. Relationship and Hierarchy
There is a direct inclusion relationship: $G_2 \subset F_4$. 

Any automorphism of the octonions automatically induces an automorphism on the Albert Algebra by acting element-wise on the matrix entries.
However, $F_4$ contains many more symmetries that mix the matrix entries in ways that do not correspond to simple octonion automorphisms. Specifically, $F_4$ acts transitively on the 26-dimensional sphere of trace-zero elements in the Albert Algebra, whereas $G_2$ acts on the 6-sphere of imaginary octonions. 
3. Role in Particle Physics
Both groups are rank-4, matching the rank of the Standard Model gauge group, but they play different theoretical roles:

$G_2$ in Physics:
Often associated with the strong force ($SU(3)$) because $SU(3)$ is a maximal subgroup of $G_2$.
Appears in M-theory compactifications: Compactifying 11-dimensional M-theory on a manifold with $G_2$ holonomy yields a 4-dimensional universe with $\mathcal{N}=1$ supersymmetry, a desirable feature for realistic particle physics models. 
$F_4$ in Physics:
Proposed as a Grand Unified Theory (GUT) candidate. Because its fundamental representation is 26-dimensional (plus the singlet identity makes 27), it naturally accommodates the three generations of fermions plus additional states.
It unifies the spacetime symmetries ($Spin(9)$) and internal gauge symmetries more tightly than $G_2$. The breaking of $F_4$ can yield the Standard Model group directly.

What is the subgroup structure of F4?


The subgroup structure of the exceptional Lie group $F_4$ (dimension 52) is characterized by its role as the automorphism group of the Albert Algebra ($H_3(\mathbb{O})$).  Its maximal subgroups fall into two main categories: those arising from the geometry of the octonionic projective plane and those related to physics-relevant symmetry breakings. 

Maximal Connected Subgroups
Up to conjugation, the maximal connected closed subgroups of the compact form of $F_4$ are:

$Spin(9)$: The double cover of $SO(9)$, with dimension 36.
Role: This is the stabilizer of a point in the octonionic projective plane $\mathbb{O}P^2$. It represents the "spacetime" symmetry in certain 10-dimensional theories.
Decomposition: The 26-dimensional representation of $F_4$ decomposes under $Spin(9)$ as $\mathbf{26} \to \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbf{9} \oplus \mathbf{16}$ (scalar, vector, and spinor). 
$Sp(3) \cdot Sp(1)$ (also denoted $(Sp(3) \times Sp(1)) / \mathbb{Z}_2$): Dimension $21 + 3 = 24$.
Role: This subgroup is related to the quaternionic substructure of the octonions. It is the centralizer of an $Sp(1)$ subgroup. 
$SU(3) \times SU(3) / \mathbb{Z}_3$: Dimension $8 + 8 = 16$.
Role: Crucial for particle physics, as this contains the Standard Model gauge group. It arises as the stabilizer of a specific idempotent element in the Albert Algebra.
Physics Connection: The intersection of $Spin(9)$ and this subgroup (or specific embeddings thereof) yields the Standard Model group $G_{SM} = SU(3)_C \times SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$. 
$G_2 \times SO(3)$ (or $G_2 \times SU(2)$): Dimension $14 + 3 = 17$.
Role: Since $G_2$ is the automorphism group of the octonions, it sits naturally inside $F_4$.  The $SO(3)$ factor rotates the three diagonal elements of the Albert matrix.

Choi matrix vs densitry matrix, with the exceptional lie algebras, and jordon and albert.


In the context of exceptional Lie algebras and the Albert Algebra ($H_3(\mathbb{O})$), the distinction between a density matrix and a Choi matrix centers on the failure of the standard tensor product structure required for the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism due to non-associativity. 

1. Density Matrices in the Albert Algebra
In the Exceptional Jordan Algebra, a density matrix is well-defined and corresponds to a physical state. 

Definition: It is an element $\rho \in H_3(\mathbb{O})$ that is positive (has non-negative real eigenvalues) and normalized ($\text{Tr}(\rho) = 1$). 
Validity: Because the Albert Algebra is power-associative, functions of a single element like $\log \rho$ and $\rho^n$ are unambiguous. This allows for a consistent definition of von Neumann entropy $S(\rho) = -\text{Tr}(\rho \log \rho)$ and Umegaki relative entropy (via eigenvalue decomposition). 
Role: These matrices represent the states of the system in "octonionic quantum mechanics," transforming under the exceptional Lie group $F_4$. 
2. The Problem with Choi Matrices
A Choi matrix represents a quantum channel (a linear map $\Phi$) as a bipartite state via the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism: $J(\Phi) = (\mathcal{I} \otimes \Phi)(|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)$.  This construction faces fundamental obstacles in the Albert Algebra:

Lack of Tensor Products: The category of Jordan algebras does not possess a natural tensor product that preserves the Jordan structure. You cannot simply form $H_3(\mathbb{O}) \otimes H_3(\mathbb{O})$ to create a composite system because the resulting algebra would not be a Jordan algebra (it fails the Jordan identity). 
Non-Associativity of Maps: The standard proof that a positive Choi matrix implies a Completely Positive (CP) map relies on the associativity of the underlying operator algebra to define the action of the map on part of an entangled system.  In the non-associative octonionic setting, the concept of "acting on a subsystem" is ill-defined without embedding the algebra into a larger associative structure.
Recent Insights: Research (e.g., Gondran, 2024; Baez & Huerta) suggests that while the Jamiołkowski variant (basis-independent) can be formulated for Jordan algebras using duality, the Choi variant (which maps CP maps to positive operators) strictly requires the associative dynamical structure of $C^*$-algebras.  For the Albert Algebra, one must often work with embedding algebras (like $M_3(\mathbb{O})$ inside $M_{24}(\mathbb{C})$) to define channels, losing the "exceptional" nature in the process.
3. Connection to Exceptional Lie Algebras
$F_4$ Symmetry: Density matrices in $H_3(\mathbb{O})$ transform naturally under $F_4$.  However, a hypothetical "Choi matrix" for a channel would live in a composite space that does not carry a simple $F_4 \times F_4$ action, complicating the description of dynamics.
$E_6$ Structure Group: The structure group of the Albert Algebra is $E_6$. While $F_4$ preserves the identity (states), $E_6$ preserves the determinant. Some approaches define generalized "channels" as elements of $E_6$ acting on the density matrix, bypassing the need for a Choi matrix representation entirely.

Choi matrix vs densitry matrix, with the exceptional lie algebras, and jordon and albert. in a noncommutative, non associative fintite space


In a noncommutative, non-associative finite space like the Albert Algebra ($H_3(\mathbb{O})$), the distinction between a density matrix and a Choi matrix becomes fundamental: the former is a well-defined state within the algebra, while the latter fails to exist as a standard operator due to the lack of a valid tensor product structure. 

1. Density Matrices: Well-Defined via Power Associativity
In the exceptional Jordan algebra, a density matrix $\rho$ is a valid physical state because its definition relies only on power associativity, which $H_3(\mathbb{O})$ possesses. 

Definition: $\rho \in H_3(\mathbb{O})$ such that $\rho \geq 0$ (non-negative eigenvalues) and $\text{Tr}(\rho) = 1$. 
Mechanism: Since powers of a single element ($\rho^n$) and functions of a single element ($\log \rho$) are unambiguous in a power-associative algebra, the spectral theorem holds. This allows for a consistent definition of entropy $S(\rho) = -\text{Tr}(\rho \log \rho)$ and Umegaki relative entropy (calculated via the eigenvalues of $\rho$ and $\sigma$).
Symmetry: These states transform naturally under the exceptional Lie group $F_4$, the automorphism group of the algebra. 
2. Choi Matrices: Undefined due to Lack of Tensor Products
A Choi matrix represents a quantum channel $\Phi$ as a bipartite state $J(\Phi) \in \mathcal{A} \otimes \mathcal{A}$.  This construction fails in the Albert Algebra for two critical reasons:

No Jordan Tensor Product: The category of Euclidean Jordan Algebras (EJAs) does not admit a natural tensor product that preserves the Jordan identity. Research (e.g., Barnum et al., 2020) proves that no non-signaling composite exists if either factor is the exceptional Jordan algebra, unless the other factor is trivial (classical).  You cannot form a composite system $H_3(\mathbb{O}) \otimes H_3(\mathbb{O})$ that remains an exceptional Jordan algebra.
Failure of Choi’s Theorem: Recent work (Frembs & Cavalcanti, 2024) demonstrates that Choi’s theorem (mapping completely positive maps to positive operators) strictly depends on the dynamical structure of $C^*$-algebras (associativity).  While the Jamiołkowski isomorphism (basis-independent) can be adapted to Jordan algebras using duality, it does not guarantee that positive maps correspond to positive "Choi operators" in the composite space because that space does not exist in the exceptional context. 
3. Implications for Exceptional Lie Algebras
$F_4$ (States): The group $F_4$ acts transitively on the space of pure states (idempotents) in $H_3(\mathbb{O})$. It describes the kinematics of single-system states perfectly. 
$E_6$ (Dynamics): The structure group of the Albert Algebra is $E_6$. In the absence of a Choi matrix representation for channels, dynamics are often modeled directly as elements of $E_6$ acting on the density matrix, rather than as maps represented by bipartite states. 
Workarounds: To define channels rigorously, one must embed $H_3(\mathbb{O})$ into an associative envelope (e.g., $M_{27}(\mathbb{C})$ or $M_3(\mathbb{O})$ viewed as a subalgebra of complex matrices). However, this breaks the "exceptional" nature of the algebra, as the composite system then belongs to a special (associative) Jordan algebra, not the exceptional one.

26-Dimensional Ball: The state space of the Albert Algebra is not a simple sphere but a convex body in 26 dimensions (the trace-zero subspace of $H_3(\mathbb{O})$).

How is Max-Plus algebra implemented computationally?


Max-Plus algebra is implemented computationally by replacing standard arithmetic operators with their tropical counterparts: addition becomes max (or min), and multiplication becomes standard +.  This simple substitution allows standard linear algebra algorithms to solve non-linear optimization problems efficiently. 

1. Core Operator Substitution
The fundamental implementation involves defining a custom semiring or simply overloading operators in code:

Tropical Addition ($\oplus$): $a \oplus b = \max(a, b)$ (or $\min$ for min-plus). 
Tropical Multiplication ($\otimes$): $a \otimes b = a + b$. 
Zero Element ($\varepsilon$): $-\infty$ (for max-plus), since $\max(a, -\infty) = a$.
One Element ($e$): $0$, since $a + 0 = a$.
In Python, this can be implemented via a simple class or by using libraries like maxpluspy or tropical-gemm:

import numpy as np

def tropical_add(A, B):
    return np.maximum(A, B)

def tropical_matmul(A, B):
    # Standard matmul: sum(A_ik * B_kj) -> Max-Plus: max(A_ik + B_kj)
    # A: (n, m), B: (m, p)
    A_exp = A[:, :, np.newaxis]  # (n, m, 1)
    B_exp = B[np.newaxis, :, :]  # (1, m, p)
    return np.max(A_exp + B_exp, axis=1)
