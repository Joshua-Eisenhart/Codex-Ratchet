## 0. Stance and role

This candidate states a conditional top-down schedule hypothesis for layer activation in a distinction-constraint manifold. It is fuel for code-driven comparison against rival schedules on finite executions. It makes no correctness claim, asserts no ontology, and admits nothing.

Ratcheting begins at the highest object-forming levels. Axes 7-12 first organize Type-1/Type-2 engine differentiation and the construction of stable multi-object structures. These high layers impose constraints backward onto the admissible realizations of axes 0-6. Axes 0-6 are treated as deep shared mechanics that become operative only where higher layers have already selected and stabilized differentiated engines and persistent objects. 13 layers total (12 down to 0). The ordering itself is a testable schedule hypothesis: higher-layer constraints are activated first; lower-layer mechanics are admitted only to the extent they are required to carry the distinctions demanded by the layers above them.

## 1. The carrier

The carrier is the ordered activation of 13 layers from 12 to 0 under top-down constraint imposition. Each layer names distinctions whose stabilization is hypothesized to precede and constrain the admissible forms of layers below it.

Layer 12: Multi-agent IGT-scale interaction surfaces. Registry of differentiated engines; joint outcomes recorded as survivor structures. Presumes stable differentiated engines and objects from layers 11-7. Persists and evolves by recording joint outcomes across engine sheets as new survivor partitions.

Layer 11: Type-1 vs Type-2 engine differentiation. Two or more engine sheets differing in handedness, flux, or H-sign. Presumes prior multi-agent registry capacity. Persists by maintaining separate engine sheets that survive distinct under the same demands.

Layer 10: Agent/perception/learning loop persistence. Fast and slow belief registries. Presumes differentiated engines. Persists and evolves by carrying loop outcomes forward as stable belief structures.

Layer 9: Physics/cosmology shell-bridge structures. Nested possibility shells with radius order. Presumes persistent agents and records. Persists by maintaining ordered shells that survive radius and direction distinctions.

Layer 8: Finite substrate carriers. Concrete finite presentations hosting higher-layer structure. Presumes shell and record structures. Persists by providing the finite substrate on which higher distinctions are realized without infinite regress.

Layer 7: History and record persistence. Record structures carrying resolved distinctions forward. Presumes finite substrates. Persists and evolves by separating record-carrying presentations from transient ones.

Layer 6: Composition precedence and action-side mechanics (Axis 6). Ordered compositions with explicit precedence and action-side, witnessing b6 = -b0*b3. Presumes persistent records. Persists by enforcing order-sensitive compositions.

Layer 5: Operator family selection (T vs F). Operators split by entropy/purity/contractivity versus orbit/spectrum preservation. Presumes composition mechanics. Persists by selecting operator families that survive matched demands on entropy versus invariance.

Layer 4: Loop traversal order (inductive vs deductive). Ordered generator sequences, Phi_D versus Phi_I; first difference is the commutator. Presumes selected operators. Persists by maintaining distinct traversal orders under commuting controls.

Layer 3: Loop class and chart-role assignment (fiber vs lifted-base). Loop presentations split by density-stationary versus density-traversing plus chart-role overlay. Presumes traversal orders. Persists by assigning and preserving fiber and base roles.

Layer 2: Representation frame (direct vs conjugated). Frames split by direct versus conjugated (transport-corrected) with isentropic invariance for pure rotations. Presumes loop classes. Persists by separating direct and conjugated frames under rotation controls.

Layer 1: Evolution branch (unitary vs proper CPTP). Evolution split by unitary versus Kraus-sum CPTP; entropy/purity kernel distinction. Presumes representation frames. Persists by maintaining separate evolution branches under purity and entropy demands.

Layer 0: Distinction gradient drive (opening vs locking polarity). Gradient presentations recording opening/locking asymmetry; Axis 0 as the gradient itself. Chart readout kept distinct from any later Phi0 bridge readout. Presumes evolution branches. Persists by recording the drive polarity as a stable gradient presentation.

## 3. Probe / distinguishability test

The probe / distinguishability test compares executions of the top-down schedule against matched controls and rival schedules on identical demand families and finite population sizes. The test measures population size, frontier cardinality under partition-refinement MSS, witnessed coface gradients, and control outcomes (whether demanded distinctions survive as additional partition classes).

Overall procedure: activate layers 12 through 7 first to establish differentiated engines, persistent objects, records, and shells. Admit layers 6 through 0 only to the extent required to carry distinctions already demanded by the higher layers. Compute the resulting MSS survivor sets. Run matched controls that force identical engines (erasure at layer 12), apply Type-1-only or Type-2-only (erasure at layer 11), reverse shell orders (layer 9), omit records (layer 7), and vary low-layer orders without high-layer constraints applied first.

Per-layer distinguishability tests:

- Layer 12: compares differentiated-registry partition versus forced-identical erasure control.
- Layer 11: compares Type-1-only, Type-2-only, and both registries for an extra partition class.
- Layer 10: compares Type-1-style versus Type-2-style loop orders for differing survivor sets.
- Layer 9: compares ordered shells versus radius-erased or direction-reversed registries.
- Layer 8: compares two substrate realizations under a fixed higher-layer demand.
- Layer 7: compares record-only versus transient-only readout after evolution.
- Layer 6: compares precedence variants under order-sensitive demand.
- Layer 5: applies matched entropy-growth versus unitary-invariance demands.
- Layer 4: compares the two traversal orders with a commuting control.
- Layer 3: compares fiber versus base assignment and chart-role flip.
- Layer 2: compares direct versus conjugated partitions plus a pure-rotation control.
- Layer 1: applies matched entropy-growth and purity demands.
- Layer 0: compares asymmetric registry versus symmetric or null-drive control.

## 4. Predictions

If the top-down ordering holds, imposing layers 12-7 first will produce stable multi-object and multi-engine structures whose demanded distinctions cannot be recovered when the same mechanics are admitted without the prior high-layer constraints. The differentiated engine registry is predicted to yield joint outcome partitions that survive only when Type-1 and Type-2 sheets are already separated. Record persistence and shell order are predicted to protect lower-layer distinctions that would otherwise collapse under pure low-layer evolution. Low-layer choices (operator families, traversal orders, evolution branches, drive polarity) are predicted to be admissible only in the forms selected by the backward constraints from above.

## 5. Rivals

Rivals are other schedule hypotheses on the same manifold, including bottom-up activation (layers 0-6 first, building objects from distinction gradients), middle-out activation (starting at layers 5-7), alternative decompositions of the 13 layers, and different carrier hypotheses that alter what counts as a stable presentation. Comparison is strictly by execution: population size, MSS frontier cardinality, coface gradients, and control outcomes on shared demand families. No schedule is presupposed.

## 6. Known failure modes -- the witness / defeat conditions

This candidate can-fail as a schedule when the imposed backward constraints from layers 7-12 produce strictly weaker or incomparable survivors than rival schedules on the same demand families. The witness for defeat is a smaller population, lower frontier cardinality under partition-refinement MSS, or reduced coface gradients (more collapsed distinction mass) in top-down runs relative to bottom-up or middle-out runs.

Specific order-risks for this candidate:

- The presupposition that stable engines and agents can be formed before low-layer distinction gradients and operator mechanics are operative leads to premature locking and loss of admissible distinctions.
- Layers 9 and 12 placed high prove to be late readouts: the same shell-bridge and multi-agent partitions emerge after low-layer mechanics without loss.
- Nonassociativity or commutator effects required at layers 4-6 are necessary to even register the handedness distinction at layer 11; top-down activation erases distinctions that bottom-up activation would preserve.
- The 7-12 object-forming versus 0-6 deep-mechanics split is an arbitrary boundary; executions show no measurable gain from enforcing that split.

The top-down ordering loses its advantage if high-layer constraints are shown to be optional rather than necessary selectors for lower mechanics.

## 7. What would flip this candidate

This candidate would flip if finite executions on matched demand families show that a bottom-up or middle-out schedule produces equal or larger MSS frontier cardinality, stronger coface gradients, and equivalent or better control outcomes without the backward imposition from layers 12-7. It would flip if engine differentiation (layer 11) and multi-agent registries (layer 12) can be reconstructed as emergent from layers 0-6 with no reduction in survivor structures. It would flip if the distinction gradient at layer 0 must be stabilized before any higher object can be distinguished without collapse. Evidence that the top-down schedule consistently yields strictly weaker survivors than rivals on identical finite populations would falsify the utility of this ordering for the ratchet.

Global weaknesses of this candidate as a whole: the top-down schedule risks presupposing the very objects, engines, and agents whose production from pure distinguishability constraints is the thing to be explained. The 13-layer object-making (7-12) versus deep-mechanics (0-6) split is a hypothesis, not a measured boundary. Nonassociativity is expected later but not demanded at any layer, a gap a rival could expose. All claims remain packet-relative and schedule-relative. Convergence under this order does not canonize the order or the layer boundaries.
